I am a PhD student in Computer Science at Oregon State University, working on Information Theory, channel coding, and machine learning. My research focuses on bringing ideas from optimization into information theory, channel coding, wireless communications, and large-scale data analysis.

During my PhD thesis in Electrical Engineering at K.N. Toosi University of Technology, I solved the open problem of finding optimal-rate LDPC codes for the binary erasure channel. My current research spans several directions: parallel stochastic quantizer architectures and threshold-learning algorithms that maximize mutual information, the capacity and input distribution of the deletion channel, and finite-blocklength information-theoretic bounds.

Before returning to graduate studies, I was an Assistant and later Associate Professor in the Electrical Engineering Department at the University of Guilan, Iran, where I led research on secure LDPC-based communication systems and mentored graduate students through publications and thesis supervision in coding theory and wireless communications.

For more details, please see my [Publications](/publications/) and [CV](/cv/). If you are interested in collaborating or have a relevant opportunity, feel free to reach out at [tavakolh@oregonstate.edu](mailto:tavakolh@oregonstate.edu).
